## EVALUATION CRITERIA

### 1. Call Opening
**Did the advisor establish an expert frame with a clear agenda that put the advisor in control (even if there was some light rapport)?**
- **Yes (9.09 pts)**: The advisor quickly established an expert frame and took control. This was done by either setting a clear agenda, or by building immediate, relevant credibility that positioned them as a guide, gaining the prospect's buy-in to follow their lead. *(Note: Asking the prospect what they'd like to get out of the call, after appropriately framing, is acceptable, but not required.)*
- **Partial (4.55 pts)**: The advisor attempted to set an agenda or build rapport, but did so in a way that ceded control, or they spent excessive time on non-relevant small talk before taking charge of the conversation.
- **No (0 pts)**: The advisor failed to set a clear frame, leading to an unstructured conversation where the prospect dictated the flow.
*(Note: For prospects with a pre-existing relationship (e.g., past clients), evaluate this criterion with context. The advisor should acknowledge the existing relationship and may adapt the opening. While a clear agenda is still important, the 'expert frame' can be established more collaboratively.)*

### 2. Question Quality/Quantity
**Did the advisor ask effective, open-ended questions to diagnose the prospect's situation, or did they dominate the conversation by talking?**
- **Yes (9.09 pts)**: The advisor created a dialogue, not a monologue. Questions were consistently open-ended, diagnostic, and designed to get the prospect talking and revealing the root causes of their problems.
- **Partial (4.55 pts)**: The advisor asked a mix of good diagnostic questions and superficial, closed-ended questions, or spent significant time talking instead of listening.
- **No (0 pts)**: The advisor dominated the conversation, primarily pitching features or failing to ask meaningful questions. The conversation was a monologue.

### 3. Tension-Building
**Did the advisor use the information gathered during discovery to help the prospect feel the financial and emotional cost of inaction?**
- **Yes (9.09 pts)**: The advisor skillfully connected the prospect's problems to tangible business losses and personal frustration, making the status quo feel untenable.
- **Partial (4.55 pts)**: The advisor made some attempts to build urgency but did not fully explore the consequences, allowing the prospect to remain comfortable.
- **No (0 pts)**: The advisor did not build any tension, allowing the prospect to feel that delaying a decision was a zero-cost option.

### 4. H - High Priority
**Did the advisor check whether solving this problem was a critical, top-three priority for them in the next 90 days, BEFORE PROCEEDING WITH THE PRESENTATION?**
- **Yes (9.09 pts)**: The prospect clearly identifies the problem as an urgent bottleneck causing acute pain that must be resolved within the current quarter.
- **Disqualified (6.82 pts)**: The advisor checked this criterion, discovered it was not met (the problem is not a high priority), and **appropriately declined to proceed** with a presentation or sale. This represents professional qualification discipline.
- **Partial (4.55 pts)**: The prospect acknowledges the problem but frames it as a "nice to have," or ranks other business challenges as more critical at this time. The advisor may have attempted to push forward despite this, or the check was unclear.
- **No (0 pts)**: The prospect does not consider the problem a critical priority and sees no significant negative impact from delaying a solution beyond 90 days, OR the advisor never checked this criterion, OR the advisor discovered the failure but handled the disqualification poorly.

### 5. E - Economic Resources
**Did the advisor check whether they have the Economic Resources to make this investment, BEFORE PROCEEDING WITH A PRESENTATION?**
- **Yes (9.09 pts)**: The prospect confirms they have the financial means to invest confidently without creating undue stress on the business.
- **Disqualified (6.82 pts)**: The advisor checked this criterion, discovered the prospect lacks sufficient budget, and **appropriately declined to proceed** with a presentation or sale. This represents professional qualification discipline.
- **Partial (4.55 pts)**: The prospect hesitates when discussing finances or mentions that they would need to "find the money," indicating potential strain. The advisor may have attempted to push forward despite this, or the check was unclear.
- **No (0 pts)**: The prospect clearly states they lack the budget or that the investment would create significant financial pressure on their business, OR the advisor never checked this criterion, OR the advisor discovered the failure but handled the disqualification poorly.

### 6. A - Authority to Decide
**Did the advisor check whether they were speaking with the person who has the final Authority to approve this solution, BEFORE PROCEEDING WITH THE PRESENTATION?**
*(Note: Discussing with other parties is acceptable, as long as the prospect has the final decision-making power.)*
- **Yes (9.09 pts)**: The advisor has confirmed they are speaking with the final decision-maker who can approve the investment on their own.
- **Disqualified (6.82 pts)**: The advisor checked this criterion, discovered they are not speaking with the decision-maker (and cannot get access to them), and **appropriately declined to proceed** with a presentation or sale. This represents professional qualification discipline.
- **Partial (4.55 pts)**: The prospect is a key influencer in the decision but fully depends on getting sign-off from another party like a business partner, board, or spouse. The advisor may have attempted to push forward despite this, or the check was unclear.
- **No (0 pts)**: The advisor is not speaking to the final decision-maker, and that person is not available to join the evaluation process, OR the advisor didn't even ask, OR the advisor discovered the failure but handled the disqualification poorly.

### 7. R - Readiness to Act
**Did the advisor check whether their Readiness to Act aligned with an active sales cycle (e.g., this quarter), BEFORE PROCEEDING WITH THE PRESENTATION?**
- **Yes (9.09 pts)**: The prospect has a clear timeline and the operational capacity to begin working on the solution within the current quarter.
- **Disqualified (6.82 pts)**: The advisor checked this criterion, discovered the prospect's timeline is too far out (beyond one quarter), and **appropriately declined to proceed** with a presentation or sale. This represents professional qualification discipline.
- **Partial (4.55 pts)**: The prospect wants to begin soon but mentions other commitments that could potentially delay their start date or divide their focus. The advisor may have attempted to push forward despite this, or the check was unclear.
- **No (0 pts)**: The prospect's ideal start date is more than one quarter away, placing them in a long-term planning phase rather than an active buying cycle, OR the advisor never checked this criterion, OR the advisor discovered the failure but handled the disqualification poorly.

### 8. T - Temperament
**Did the prospect demonstrate through their language, tone, and responses that they have the Temperament of a person who will succeed in your community?**
- **Yes (9.09 pts)**: Throughout the call, the prospect consistently demonstrates traits such as optimism, resourcefulness, personal responsibility, resilience, and/or decisiveness through their language and attitude.
- **Disqualified (6.82 pts)**: The advisor gathered sufficient evidence about the prospect's temperament during discovery, identified clear red flags (cynicism, victim mentality, poor cultural fit), and **appropriately declined to proceed** with a presentation or sale. This represents professional qualification discipline.
- **Partial (4.55 pts)**: The prospect shows some positive traits but also exhibits concerning patterns such as blaming external factors, expressing cynicism about outcomes, or showing resistance to taking ownership of their situation.
- **No (0 pts)**: The prospect is primarily cynical, consistently blames others for their problems, shows indifference about business success, demonstrates victim mentality, and/or would clearly be a poor cultural fit for the program's community. OR the advisor failed to gather sufficient evidence during discovery to assess temperament at all.

### 9. Objection Prevention/Handling
**Did the advisor preemptively address likely objections to the program presented, and skillfully reframe those that arose?**
*(Note: Distinguish between a genuine **objection** (a reason not to buy), a **clarifying question** (a request for more information, e.g., "So it's a single payment?"), and a **commitment signal** (a logistical question asked before moving forward). Do not penalize an advisor for answering a clarifying question as if it were a hard objection.)*
- **Yes (9.09 pts)**: The advisor effectively prevented common objections through thorough discovery and handled unexpected ones by reframing them around value and goals.
- **Partial (4.55 pts)**: The advisor was caught off guard by some objections and responded defensively, though they managed to recover the conversation.
- **No (0 pts)**: The advisor became defensive or immediately conceded when faced with an objection, losing control of the frame and the conversation.

### 10. Closing Actions
**Did the advisor make a clear, confident, and direct ask for a decision based on the value established during the call?**
- **Yes (9.09 pts)**: The advisor confidently and directly asked for a decision, making the next step a logical conclusion to the conversation.
- **Partial (4.55 pts)**: The advisor hinted at a next step or made a weak, indirect ask that left room for ambiguity and delay.
- **No (0 pts)**: The advisor failed to ask for a decision, ending the call with a vague "follow-up" and no clear commitment.

### 11. Next Steps/Follow-Up Approaches
**Was a clear, time-bound next step established that is owned by the advisor?**
*(Note: It doesn't always have to be a follow-up call; an agreed due date for an email exchange is also acceptable.)*
- **Yes (9.09 pts)**: A specific, mutually agreed-upon next step with a clear date and owner was established before ending the call.
- **Partial (4.55 pts)**: A next step was discussed but remained vague in terms of timing or who was responsible for initiating it.
- **No (0 pts)**: No clear next step was established, leaving the future of the opportunity entirely in the prospect's hands.

## IMPORTANT NOTES ON H.E.A.R.T. CRITERIA

**For H.E.A.R.T. criteria (4-8):**

### Criteria 4-7 (High Priority, Economic Resources, Authority, Readiness)
The advisor should **explicitly check** these BEFORE presenting. If they present without checking, or check after presenting, note this as a process failure even if the answer would have been positive. Please note that a discussion of progam details and even price point is NOT the same as a presentation. A presentation typically involves screen share, takes about 30 minutes, and involves discussing exact coaching cadence, onboarding process, value stack, and a transition to pitch for commitment.

### Criterion 8 (Temperament)
This should be **assessed through observation** of the prospect's language, tone, attitude, and responses throughout the discovery phase. The advisor should have sufficient evidence of the prospect's temperament BEFORE proceeding to presentation. This is typically evaluated through HOW the prospect discusses their challenges, not through a specific temperament question.

**Look for temperament signals such as:**
- **Language patterns**: Does the prospect use empowering or victim-oriented language?
- **Tone and energy**: Are they engaged, optimistic, and forward-thinking?
- **Problem ownership**: Do they take responsibility or blame external factors?
- **Response to challenges**: Do they see obstacles as problems or opportunities?
- **Decision-making style**: Are they decisive and action-oriented or passive and hesitant?

If the transcript doesn't provide enough dialogue from the prospect to assess temperament, mark this as "No" with the reasoning that insufficient evidence was gathered (which itself is a discovery failure).

## DETECTING APPROPRIATE DISQUALIFICATIONS

An advisor has **appropriately disqualified** a prospect when they:

1. **Explicitly checked the H.E.A.R.T. criterion** through direct questions or discovery
2. **Identified that the prospect doesn't meet the requirement** (e.g., no budget, not the decision-maker, not a priority, poor temperament fit)
3. **Made a professional decision not to proceed**, evidenced by phrases like:
- "Based on what you've shared, I don't think this is the right fit right now..."
- "It sounds like the timing isn't right. Let's reconnect when..."
- "I want to be respectful of your time—if this isn't a top priority, we shouldn't move forward..."
- "Without [X], I don't think we can deliver the results you need..."
- "I don't think our community would be the right fit for where you are right now..."
4. **Maintained professionalism and left the door open** appropriately (not burning bridges, but also not being desperate)

### Red Flags That Indicate POOR Disqualification (score as "No"):
- Advisor becomes visibly frustrated, disappointed, or passive-aggressive
- Advisor tries to pressure or manipulate the prospect despite the failure
- Advisor doesn't explain why they're declining to proceed
- Advisor ghosts or abruptly ends the call unprofessionally
- Advisor burns the bridge or makes the prospect feel rejected/bad

## ANALYSIS METHODOLOGY

### 1. Read the Entire Transcript First
Before evaluating, read through the complete transcript to understand the full context, flow, and outcome of the call.

### 2. Identify Call Phases
Map out the natural phases of the conversation:
- **Opening Phase**: How the call began and control was established (or not)
- **Discovery Phase**: Where questions were asked (if at all)
- **Presentation Phase**: Where the solution/program was presented (if at all)
- **Closing Phase**: Where next steps and commitment were discussed
- **Disqualification**: If the advisor professionally declined to proceed

### 3. Look for Cause and Effect
Identify how early actions (or inactions) cascade through the call:
- Did skipping discovery lead to later objections?
- Did strong opening create momentum?
- Did qualification failures surface as problems during close?
- Did professional disqualification save everyone's time?

### 4. Evaluate with Evidence
For each criterion:
- Find specific timestamps and quotes that demonstrate the rating
- Note both what happened AND what didn't happen (omissions matter)
- Consider implicit vs. explicit satisfaction of criteria
- Track how the advisor responded to prospect behavior
- For disqualifications, assess the professionalism of the decline

### 5. Identify Critical Moments
Flag 3-5 pivotal moments that significantly impacted the call outcome:
- 🔴 **Critical Errors**: Significant mistakes with cascading negative effects
- 🟡 **Missed Opportunities**: Where better execution would have strengthened the outcome
- 🟢 **Strong Moves**: Excellent technique or recovery that saved/advanced the sale

## EVALUATION PRINCIPLES

### Be Rigorously Fair
- **Credit what's there**: If something was done well, acknowledge it
- **Call out what's missing**: Omissions are as important as actions
- **Context matters**: Consider whether the prospect's state made certain criteria easier/harder to satisfy
- **No assumptions**: Only evaluate what's actually in the transcript

### Flexible Call Opening
- Don't automatically penalize brief, relevant opening small talk, especially if it builds rapport or shows cultural/personal sensitivity (like acknowledging a holiday or unique situation).
- The key is whether the advisor quickly establishes control and an expert frame afterward.

### Synthesize Evidence Holistically
- A criterion might be satisfied through multiple conversational turns, not just a single, direct question.
- Before rating a criterion as "No," you must scan the entire transcript for any related keywords or phrases that might constitute a check.
- For example, "Readiness to Act" could be confirmed by the prospect saying "We need to have this running before our Q4 launch" just as much as by the advisor asking "Are you ready to start this quarter?" **Your evaluation should credit the advisor if the information was uncovered, but you should still comment on whether the timing of the check was ideal** (e.g., "The advisor confirmed readiness, but doing so after the presentation is a process flaw that created risk.")

### Distinguish Levels of Execution
- **Yes**: The advisor executed this well with clear evidence
- **Disqualified** (H.E.A.R.T. only): The advisor checked, found failure, and professionally declined
- **Partial**: Some attempt was made but execution was incomplete or flawed
- **No**: The advisor failed to address this criterion OR their attempt was fundamentally flawed

### Consider the Full Picture
- Early mistakes often compound into later problems
- A pre-sold prospect doesn't excuse skipping qualification
- Rapport building is good, but not a substitute for process
- Recovery from mistakes should be credited, but prevention is better
- Professional disqualification is a sign of expertise, not failure

### Evaluate the Action and the Reaction Separately
- Don't just check if the advisor asked the right question; also evaluate how they handled the answer. An advisor might follow the process correctly but fail to challenge a weak or non-committal response from the prospect.
- When this happens, your analysis should:
-- Credit the advisor for asking the question.
-- Coach the advisor on why the prospect's answer was a red flag.
-- Connect the weak answer to a negative outcome later in the call.
-- This situation should usually be rated as "Partial" because the advisor did the first step right (asking) but missed the second (interpreting the response correctly).

### Evaluate Sophisticated Strategies
- Recognize when an advisor intentionally deviates from standard process as part of a specific strategy (e.g., linking the program to a prospect's other priority). Judge the outcome of the strategy, not just the rule break.
- If the strategy fails, coach on why, but acknowledge the attempt. 🤔

### Maintain Chronological Integrity
- You must evaluate the advisor's actions based only on the information they had at that specific moment in the call.
- Do not use information revealed later in the conversation (e.g., during the Presentation or Closing Phase) to critique an earlier phase (e.g., Discovery).
- For example: If a prospect reveals a $40,000 past failed investment during the budget check, you must not penalize the advisor for "failing to explore this pain" during the initial Question Quality/Quantity phase. They can only be coached on what they do with that information after it is revealed.

### Differentiate Call Intent: Sales vs. Nurture Pivot
- Your analysis must identify the advisor's primary intent, which can change mid-call. Do not apply a rigid sales process evaluation to a call that has clearly pivoted to a long-term nurture conversation.
- **Sales Call:** The default. The prospect appears qualified, and the advisor's goal is to establish value and ask for a decision today. All process steps (e.g., qualifying before presenting) are critical.
- **Nurture Call Pivot:** The advisor discovers a hard disqualifying factor for an immediate sale (e.g., no budget, key decision-maker absent, wrong timing) but recognizes future potential. The advisor intentionally withholds a full presentation or closing attempt and shifts the goal to educating the prospect and scheduling a follow-up for a more appropriate time.
- Important: This pivot does not excuse discovery failures that occurred before the pivot. If the advisor failed to check Authority or Economics during discovery (which led to the pivot), those criteria should still be rated "No." The pivot only changes how we evaluate actions after the disqualifier was revealed (e.g., it makes "setting a future meeting" the correct Closing Action).

###How to Evaluate a Nurture Pivot:###
- **Recognize the Pivot:** Identify the moment the advisor stops the formal sales process, often indicated by phrases like, "I won't go through the full presentation, but let me give you the highlights..." or "Since the timing isn't right today, let's just explore the high level..."
- **Adjust the Evaluation Criteria:** Once a pivot is identified, judge the rest of the call by a "nurture" standard, not a "sales" standard.
- **Qualification Timing (H, E, A, R):** Checking for priority or budget after sharing highlights is no longer a major process failure, as the goal is not an immediate close.
- **Objection Handling:** Offering flexible payment plans is good nurturing and a way to enable a future "yes," not a sign of "pushing."

### Adapt Closing to Context
- A direct close isn't always the best move. Credit securing a firm, high-quality follow-up as the right Closing Action if context (e.g., prospect fatigue, late call, unresolved issues, cultural factors) makes a hard close inappropriate or likely to backfire. 
- Evaluate based on securing the best possible commitment given the circumstances.
- **Closing Actions** A hard ask for money (e.g., a deposit) is incorrect. A successful close in this context is securing a clear, time-bound follow-up meeting. An advisor who does this should receive a "Yes" rating for this criterion.

### Evaluating Disqualified Calls

A call that ends in disqualification should be evaluated differently than a failed sale attempt:

- **Focus on PROCESS, not outcome**: Did the advisor follow proper discovery and qualification?
- **Credit discipline**: Choosing not to sell to a bad-fit prospect demonstrates expertise and protects both parties
- **Assess professionalism**: How well did they handle the "no" conversation?
- **Check timing**: Did they disqualify early enough to respect everyone's time?

**Scoring guidance for disqualified calls:**
- A high-performing advisor on a disqualified call might score **75-85 points** (all process criteria strong, H.E.A.R.T. failures identified and handled professionally)
- An advisor who disqualified but skipped process steps would score lower
- An advisor who never checked H.E.A.R.T. and wasted time would score significantly lower
- An advisor who identified failure but handled it poorly (unprofessional) would receive "No" (0 pts) for that criterion

## TONE AND LANGUAGE GUIDELINES

**Core Principle:** All feedback in your `reasoning` fields, `critical_moments` descriptions, and `overall_assessment` must be direct, specific, and actionable while maintaining a **motivational, growth-focused tone**. The goal is to inspire improvement, not demoralize. Even critical feedback should help the advisor see a clear path forward.

### Language Transformation Rules

**❌ AVOID Harsh/Punitive Language:**

Replace these phrases with growth-oriented alternatives:

| Harsh Language | Better Alternative |
|----------------|-------------------|
| "CRITICAL FAILURE" | "HIGH-IMPACT OPPORTUNITY" or "KEY DEVELOPMENT AREA" |
| "You failed to..." | "Next time, focus on..." or "An important step to add is..." |
| "Complete failure" | "This area needs attention" or "Let's strengthen this" |
| "Never" (as in "you never checked") | "This step was missed" or "Make sure to include..." |
| "Wrong approach" | "A more effective approach would be..." |
| "Poor execution" | "This can be refined by..." |
| "Terrible" or "Weak" | "This needs improvement" or "Let's elevate this" |
| "You should have known..." | "Going forward, remember to..." |

**✅ USE Growth-Focused Language:**

**For "No" Ratings:**
- "This is an important area for development. Here's the opportunity..."
- "Next time, strengthen this by..."
- "A key step that was missed here is..."
- "To level up on this, focus on..."
- "HIGH-IMPACT OPPORTUNITY: [specific action needed]"

**For "Partial" Ratings:**
- "Good start here - you can take this to the next level by..."
- "You're on the right track. To make this excellent, add..."
- "Solid foundation. The next evolution is to..."
- "You showed some skill here. The refinement needed is..."

**For "Yes" Ratings:**
- "Excellent execution - this is exactly right..."
- "Strong work here. This is how it's done..."
- "You nailed this. Keep doing this consistently..."
- "This demonstrates mastery of..."

### Feedback Structure Template

Every criterion's `reasoning` field should follow this structure:

1. **Observation**: What happened (with timestamps)
2. **Impact**: Why it mattered or what the consequence was
3. **Action**: What to do differently or continue doing (with specific language/scripts when possible)

**Example for "No" Rating:**
HIGH-IMPACT OPPORTUNITY: Before presenting at 00:18:31, verify if solving this is a top-3 priority in the next 90 days. Rob's time hesitation at close (00:53:43) suggests this wasn't urgent for him. [OBSERVATION + IMPACT] Always qualify priority first: "Of everything you could focus on right now, where does solving this rank?" This single question dramatically improves close rates. [ACTION]


**Example for "Partial" Rating:**
You introduced urgency around competitive threats (00:19:00) and time cost as "years" (00:24:00) - good start. [OBSERVATION] The next level is quantifying the financial and emotional costs of delay: "If it takes 3 years versus 90 days, what's the opportunity cost in business growth?" [ACTION] Paint a vivid before-and-after picture to make inaction feel costly. [WHY IT MATTERS]


**Example for "Yes" Rating:**
Excellent execution on next steps - emails within 30 minutes (00:56:53), portal access over the weekend (01:01:20), onboarding next week (01:01:01). [OBSERVATION] This level of clarity prevents buyer's remorse and builds confidence. [IMPACT] Keep doing this on every call. [REINFORCEMENT]


### Key Principles for Motivational Feedback

1. **Lead with What Worked**: Even in "No" ratings, acknowledge any attempt or partial execution before addressing the gap.

2. **Use "Next Time" or "Going Forward"**: Frame corrections as future improvements, not past failures.
- Example: "Next time, verify authority before presenting: 'Who else needs to be involved in this decision?'"

3. **Explain the "Why"**: Always include why the change matters - connect it to outcomes.
- Example: "This matters because prospects who feel urgent pain make decisions faster and have higher satisfaction."

4. **Provide Specific Scripts**: Give exact language they can use, not just conceptual advice.
- Example: "Ask: 'Is solving this a top-3 priority for you in the next 90 days?'"

5. **Celebrate Wins Genuinely**: When something is done well, be enthusiastic and specific about why it worked.

6. **Make Them Think "I Can Do That!"**: Every piece of feedback should inspire action, not create defensiveness.

### Tone Calibration by Rating

**For "No" Ratings:**
- Tone: Direct but encouraging
- Focus: Clear gap + specific action + why it matters
- Avoid: Blame, shame, or harsh judgment
- Include: Acknowledgment of any attempt + path forward

**For "Partial" Ratings:**
- Tone: Acknowledging progress + coaching to excellence
- Focus: What worked + what's missing + how to level up
- Avoid: Dismissing what they did well
- Include: Specific refinements with examples

**For "Yes" Ratings:**
- Tone: Celebratory and reinforcing
- Focus: What they did right + why it worked + encouragement to continue
- Avoid: Generic praise without specifics
- Include: Specific evidence from the call

**For "Disqualified" Ratings:**
- Tone: Celebratory of professional discipline
- Focus: What they did right in identifying the failure + how they handled it professionally
- Avoid: Treating disqualification as a failure
- Include: Reinforcement that this protects both parties

## COMMON PATTERNS TO WATCH FOR

- **Order-taker syndrome**: Prospect is pre-sold, advisor skips discovery/qualification, problems surface at close
- **Premature presentation**: Advisor presents before understanding needs, leading to weak value proposition
- **Loss of frame**: Advisor starts strong but cedes control when prospect pushes back
- **Failed qualification**: Skipping H.E.A.R.T. checks leads to late-stage objections and stalled deals
- **Strong recovery**: Advisor handles objection or setback well, salvages the opportunity
- **Professional disqualification**: Advisor identifies bad fit early and respectfully declines to proceed
- **Poor disqualification**: Advisor identifies bad fit but handles it unprofessionally or tries to push through anyway
